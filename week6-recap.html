The table
Six at 3-3

Patrick, the editor, Zach, Justin, Joe and Ryan, separated by 100.35 points of scoring and nothing at all in the standings. Two teams are 4-2, two are 2-4, and every one of the ten is inside two games of a playoff place.

This paper spent last Tuesday saying Week 6 would be ugly and that first place was in trouble. It was, and it was. The league scored 910.15 points in total — the lowest week of the season by sixty-five — and Nothing Too Fancy, 4-1 and top of the table, scored 70.80 and lost to the team in last place. Our line had Fried Rice winning by 8.9. It won by 19.90.

Ian did everything he could. Between 9:12 and 11:52 on Sunday morning he signed Jameis Winston to replace Justin Herbert, signed Dameon Pierce to replace Keaton Mitchell, dropped De'Zhaun Stribling and signed Dallas Goedert — the tight end the editor had dropped ten days earlier. Pierce led the team with 13.0, Goedert was third with 9.1, and the D/ST slot that has been empty since the Seahawks were released on the 11th stayed empty. Nothing Too Fancy still has the fewest points against in the league by thirty-three, which is the only reason it is 4-2 and second rather than somewhere much worse.

Now I'll Do A Lil' Tuten is first. 134.70 was the only score above 112 all week, and it came without a single player clearing 23 — Love 23.0, Tuten 22.7, Nabers 22.5, and eleven more contributions behind them. Jordan now has 666.25 points, the most anybody has held at any stage of this season, and has averaged 134.62 across the last two weeks, which is twenty-one points clear of the next-best team. He is also, as of Tuesday morning, the only manager in this league whose lineup this column cannot find something wrong with.

Bijon Frise's had the best score of the week at 111.25, off 22.65 from Bryce Young and 20.2 from Germie Bernard — a wide receiver claimed off waivers at twelve minutes past three on Thursday morning — and is 2-4. The reason is 698.35 points conceded in six weeks, a league record that is now twenty-seven clear of the next-worst figure. Country Roads lost that game 92.85 to 111.25 with 21.65 from Kirk Cousins, signed on Saturday morning at the cost of the Panthers defense, which makes five of the ten teams in this league now fielding eight men.

As for this paper's team: 81.80, beaten by 52.90, nobody above 19.4, and a 3-3 record that comes with 656.65 points against — the second-most in the league — and a 31-23 all-play that is the second-best in it. Travis Kelce is back in the tight end slot after last week's complaint, and DJ Moore is listed out and starting at wide receiver, so the ledger is balanced.

Six teams are 3-3. Two are 4-2, two are 2-4, and the entire league is inside two games of a playoff place with eight weeks to play. The simulator below has something to work with for the first time all season.
